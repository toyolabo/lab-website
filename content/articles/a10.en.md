## Overview

This research program builds an integrated platform for large-scale distributed training of graph neural networks (GNNs) on supercomputers. Real-world graphs — financial transaction networks, web graphs, brain connectivity — reach billions of vertices and hundreds of billions of edges, exceeding the memory of any single GPU or node. The program integrates graph partitioning, distributed training algorithms, communication optimization, and supercomputer architecture exploitation to make GNNs and graph foundation models trainable at this scale. It is where all three of the lab's axes — graphs, AI, and HPC — intersect.

## Highlights

- Research program building distributed GNN training infrastructure on supercomputers for graphs of billions of vertices and hundreds of billions of edges
- Integrates accumulated technologies: world #1 Graph500 BFS (2016), trillion-edge partitioning (PVLDB 2019), 30x-faster distributed dynamic-GNN training (SC 2021)
- The intersection of all three lab axes: graphs, AI, and HPC

## Challenges

Distributed GNN training differs qualitatively from standard data/model parallelism: samples are not independent, node representations require neighbor information, so any partition incurs cross-boundary communication — dominated by hub-node neighborhood explosion in power-law graphs. Dynamic graphs add data movement along the time axis.

The lab has accumulated the building blocks: communication optimization proven by the #1 Graph500 BFS result (IEEE BigData 2016), trillion-edge distributed edge partitioning (PVLDB 2019), and up to 30x speedup in distributed dynamic-GNN training (SC 2021).

## Research Directions

Current work integrates these into: (1) partitioning and placement theory/implementations that minimize training-time communication; (2) distributed training algorithms exploiting supercomputer interconnects — communication-computation overlap, gradient and feature compression; (3) performance evaluation and tuning on real systems such as Fugaku and GPU supercomputers; (4) running the large-scale pre-training required by the lab's Graph Foundation Model program.

## Positioning

Just as language models advanced hand-in-hand with distributed training infrastructure, graph foundation models require scalable training platforms for graphs. This program provides that computational backbone while contributing original HPC research (SC, PVLDB, and beyond).

## Outlook

As language models advanced inseparably from training infrastructure (Megatron-LM, DeepSpeed), graph foundation models require scalable training platforms for graphs. Distributed GNN training differs qualitatively from standard parallelism — neighborhood aggregation generates communication under any partitioning, hub-node explosion dominates in power-law graphs, and mini-batch neighbor sampling and feature-tensor transfer stress systems unlike matrix-centric HPC workloads. The program pursues communication-minimizing partitioning theory, supercomputer-tuned distributed algorithms (communication-computation overlap, gradient/feature compression), and evaluation on real systems from Fugaku to GPU supercomputers — providing the computational backbone for the GFM program while contributing original HPC research, with spillover to scientific computing and industrial-scale graph analytics.
