## Overview

This work reports the design and trial operation of the ARIM-mdx Data System, a nationwide data platform for materials science in Japan that enables researchers to seamlessly integrate data from leading-edge experimental facilities and supercomputers. Its hybrid architecture combines peta-scale dedicated high-performance storage, computational resources of mdx, a platform for building data-empowered society, and IoT devices that transfer data even from network-disabled instruments. In its trial phase the platform involved 11 universities and institutes and grew to 800+ researchers (900+ as of December 2024) from ~140 academic and industrial organizations. Presented at IEEE BigData 2024 (Washington DC); a collaboration of UTokyo (ITC and School of Engineering), ISM, JAEA, Hiroshima University, and RIKEN, first-authored by our lab's Project Lecturer Masatoshi Hanai.

- A materials-science data platform that connects 40+ shared-use instruments at the University of Tokyo and transfers data from nine supercomputers in Japan, including Fugaku (IEEE BigData 2024)
- Hybrid architecture — peta-scale dedicated storage + mdx + RxT IoT devices — balances security, scalability, and agility

## Background

In the ML/AI era, the synergy of theory and experiment is essential to materials science: theoretical data from DFT and MD on supercomputers predict structures and properties, while experimental data come from electron microscopes and X-ray instruments — e.g., nanostructure analysis pairs atomic-scale microscopy images with compute-intensive simulation. Large-scale management of computational data is well established, but nationwide collection of raw experimental data remained a challenge: prior efforts covered only smaller cross-institutional or single-domain scopes or article-curated data, leaving vast raw data untouched.

## System Design

Four requirements drive the design. Stability and security: materials data can include confidential information on hazardous or economically significant materials, so the system rests on peta-scale dedicated storage under full physical control, with transfer offloaded to IoT devices to protect network-disabled facility computers. Scalability and efficiency: state-of-the-art cryo-EM generates TB-scale data per material; a hybrid architecture separates computation (on general-purpose resources of mdx) from the isolated storage. Agility and interdisciplinarity: fluctuating demands — batch supercomputer workloads plus interactive analysis during experiments — are served by high-performance interactive cloud resources with fast network connections to supercomputers.

## Impact

Within a year of trial operation, 11 universities and institutes took part and 800+ researchers (900+ as of December 2024) from ~140 organizations used the platform. More than 40 shared-use instruments at the University of Tokyo are connected, and large-scale data transfer from nine supercomputers in Japan, including Fugaku, has been achieved. As a pioneering platform that integrates experimental and computational data at national scale, it has the potential to serve as a reference design for large-scale materials-science data platforms. It was developed as part of MEXT's ARIM Japan project, and further expansion of facilities and users is planned.

## Outlook

As part of MEXT's ARIM Japan project, the ARIM-mdx Data System continues to expand its connected facilities and user base. By integrating experimental and simulation data nationwide, measurement data once scattered across labs and instruments can be accumulated as data usable for machine learning. As future directions, the paper lists long-term data preservation, publication, and sharing in line with the FAIR principles (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable), and complementing sparse data by fusing open data, academic literature, and large language models (LLMs).
