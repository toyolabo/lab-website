## Overview

This flagship research program pursues Graph Foundation Models (GFMs) — large-scale pre-trained models transferable to graph data in any domain, as LLMs are for language. Financial transactions, molecules, EEG sensor networks, road networks, social networks: all relational data are graphs, yet today's graph ML trains separate models per domain and task. GFMs aim to acquire universal structural patterns of graphs through large-scale pre-training, adapting to diverse domains and tasks with little data — a general-purpose AI substrate.

## Highlights

- Flagship program pursuing Graph Foundation Models — large pre-trained models transferable to graph data in any domain, as LLMs are for language
- Integrates the lab’s portfolio: dynamic graph theory (EvolveGCN, wavelets, EFT), spectral expressivity analysis, domain applications, and distributed training infrastructure
- Advances along three fronts: cross-domain pre-training, graph × LLM integration, and supercomputer-scale training

## Why Graph Foundation Models

LLMs showed that self-supervised pre-training on massive text captures universal linguistic patterns that transfer broadly. Whether the same holds for graphs is a major open problem. Graphs lack language's shared vocabulary and sequence structure: node/edge semantics, features, and scales differ per domain. Learning something common to molecular graphs and financial networks demands structure-centric representations independent of domain features, alignment across feature spaces, and architectures that absorb scale differences.

## Program Components

The program integrates the lab's research portfolio: dynamic graph representation learning (EvolveGCN; AAAI 2023 wavelets; ICLR 2024 EFT) provides the theory of temporal evolution; spectral expressivity analysis (TMLR 2022) guides architecture design. Domain research — the EEG foundation model (GEFM), financial fraud detection, traffic forecasting, recommender systems — supplies both downstream transfer targets and testbeds for cross-domain structure. The distributed GNN training platform on supercomputers (SC 2021 and the ongoing program) provides the computational backbone for large-scale pre-training.

## Vision

Under the lab's vision of "understanding intelligence through relationships," the GFM program advances along three fronts: (1) cross-domain graph pre-training methods, (2) graph × LLM integration (injecting structural information into language models), and (3) supercomputer-scale training. A general graph AI that transfers across fields would become foundational technology for materials, healthcare, finance, and cities alike.

## Outlook

Fundamental open questions remain: What transferable structure do graphs from different domains share? Do LLM-style scaling laws hold for graphs? How best to inject structural information into LLMs? Our lab approaches these from two distinctive strengths — spectral representations (domain-agnostic frequency descriptions) and distributed training infrastructure (compute for large-scale pre-training). GEFM and EEG-GraphAdapter are early demonstrations in neuroscience; fraud detection, traffic, recommendation, and materials informatics research provide transfer targets and testbeds. Internationally, GFMs are a fast-moving frontier of ML research, and our combination of dynamic graphs, spectral theory, and HPC gives the lab a unique position — with general graph AI promising impact across drug discovery, materials design, finance, and cities.
